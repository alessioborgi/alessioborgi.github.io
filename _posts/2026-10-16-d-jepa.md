<div class="tldr-box"><strong>TL;DR:</strong> D-JEPA combines a feature-prediction objective with a conditional denoising objective. Predicted representations guide the generation of missing continuous tokens. It is a generative extension of JEPA, so its output and evaluation differ from a representation-only encoder.</div>

{% include paper-preview.html paper="arxiv-2410-03755" %}

## A useful feature does not specify every detail

**Intuition first.** Visible image regions may suggest that a hidden region contains fabric without determining its exact texture. A predicted feature can describe the context for a distribution of plausible completions. A generative model then supplies a way to sample one.

[Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03755) build D-JEPA around this separation. A context encoder and feature predictor estimate representations at masked positions. A conditional denoising network models continuous tokens using those predictions. Feature matching and generative denoising are trained together; the paper also explores a flow-matching alternative.

## Keep the token and its predicted representation separate

Let $$x_i$$ denote an input token and $$z_i$$ the context-derived feature used to condition its generation. In the ImageNet implementation, input tokens come from a pretrained VAE, so both quantities live in latent spaces with different jobs. [Official implementation](https://github.com/D-JEPA/djepa-imagenet).

For a noise-prediction formulation,

<div class="formula-box">
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_i^t&=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_i+
\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon,\\
\mathcal L_d&=\mathbb E\left[\|\epsilon-
\epsilon_\theta(x_i^t,t,z_i)\|_2^2\right],\\
\mathcal L&=\mathcal L_d+\mathcal L_p.
\end{aligned}
\]
</div>

$$\epsilon$$ is Gaussian noise, $$t$$ is diffusion time, and $$\mathcal L_p$$ matches predicted features to target-encoder features. The [paper's formulation](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03755v2) specifies the feature loss and the two objectives. Diffusion time here is a noise level, not a video's physical timestamp.

## Worked example: reconstruct one noisy coordinate

Take a scalar clean token $$x=2$$, sampled noise $$\epsilon=-1$$, and $$\bar\alpha_t=0.64$$. Its corrupted value is

<div class="formula-box">
\[
x^t=0.8(2)+0.6(-1)=1.0.
\]
</div>

If the denoiser predicts $$\hat\epsilon=-0.8$$, its squared noise error is $$(-1+0.8)^2=0.04$$. An estimate of the clean coordinate is

<div class="formula-box">
\[
\hat x=\frac{x^t-0.6\hat\epsilon}{0.8}
=\frac{1+0.48}{0.8}=1.85.
\]
</div>

An exact noise prediction would recover 2 in this idealised calculation. This is one training example at one noise level; it is not the complete reverse sampling procedure.

The conditioning feature $$z_i$$ helps distinguish which clean values are plausible in context. It is not itself the sampled noise or the clean token being recovered.

## Sampling has two kinds of progress

The model gradually fills sets of masked token positions. Within a selected position, a conditional generative procedure produces the continuous token value. Filling additional positions supplies new context for subsequent predictions.

Consequently, the number of outer token-filling iterations and the number of denoising evaluations are different quantities. Compare both when interpreting generation cost. A smaller prediction network does not, by itself, establish lower end-to-end latency.

## Compare the output required by the task

For an image generator, evaluate visual quality, diversity, conditioning, and computational cost. For a representation learner, evaluate what downstream predictors can extract from its features. Those measurements are related but not interchangeable.

A generated sample can look plausible while differing from an original hidden region, since multiple completions may be valid. Conversely, a useful representation may omit detail needed for faithful reconstruction. This makes the choice of evaluation especially important when comparing D-JEPA with [I-JEPA](/blog/jepa/i-jepa/).

<div class="insight-box"><strong>Key Insight:</strong> D-JEPA assigns representation prediction and conditional generation separate roles. The predicted feature supplies context; the generative objective models variation in the token values consistent with that context.</div>

Compare [LLM-JEPA](/blog/jepa/llm-jepa/) for another combination of representation prediction and a generative training objective.
