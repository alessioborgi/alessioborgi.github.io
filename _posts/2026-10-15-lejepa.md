<div class="tldr-box"><strong>TL;DR:</strong> Making related views agree is insufficient: constant embeddings agree perfectly. LeJEPA combines a prediction objective with Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg), which shapes the distribution of embeddings. Its theoretical claims depend on the paper's assumptions about downstream prediction; a Gaussian-shaped feature cloud alone does not establish semantic understanding.</div>

{% include paper-preview.html paper="arxiv-2511-08544" %}

## Agreement has a trivial solution

**Intuition first.** Ask a model to give two photographs of the same object similar representations. Returning the same vector for every photograph satisfies the instruction. The loss needs a second requirement that distinguishes useful agreement from this shortcut.

[Balestriero and LeCun](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544) propose LeJEPA, pairing prediction with a regulariser that pushes embeddings towards an isotropic Gaussian distribution. Their formulation trains without the moving-average teacher or stop-gradient used in several earlier JEPA recipes.

## Two terms with different jobs

In the paper's image-view formulation, let $$z_{n,v}$$ be the embedding of view $$v$$ of sample $$n$$, and $$\bar z_n$$ the mean embedding of its global views. A compact expression is

<div class="formula-box">
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pred}}&=\frac{1}{NVd}
\sum_{n,v}\|z_{n,v}-\bar z_n\|_2^2,\\
\mathcal L&=(1-\lambda)\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pred}}
+\lambda\mathcal L_{\mathrm{SIGReg}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
</div>

Here $$d$$ is feature dimension; SIGReg is evaluated across samples for each view and then averaged. The centre remains part of the differentiable computation. See the [method and implementation](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08544v3) and [official repository](https://github.com/galilai-group/lejepa).

Prediction ties together related observations. Regularisation constrains the population of representations. Neither role can be inferred from the other term's scalar value.

## How random projections reveal a collapsed cloud

SIGReg compares projected embedding distributions to a standard Gaussian using characteristic functions. For a unit direction $$u$$ and projected samples $$s_n=u^\top z_n$$, the empirical characteristic function is

<div class="formula-box">
\[
\hat\varphi_u(t)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{it s_n},
\qquad \varphi_{\mathcal N(0,1)}(t)=e^{-t^2/2}.
\]
</div>

The method aggregates discrepancies over frequencies and sampled directions. This checks more than means and variances; the paper supplies the statistical construction and its assumptions. [SIGReg derivation](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08544v3).

## Worked example: the zero embedding

If every embedding is zero, every projection is zero. Hence $$\hat\varphi_u(t)=1$$ for every direction and frequency. At $$t=1$$, the target value is $$e^{-1/2}\approx0.6065$$. The squared discrepancy at that single frequency is

<div class="formula-box">
\[
|1-e^{-1/2}|^2\approx0.1548.
\]
</div>

The prediction term may be zero while this distribution discrepancy is positive. This calculation illustrates the mechanism; it is not the complete SIGReg loss, which aggregates and weights its comparisons.

Now consider embeddings drawn independently of their images from a Gaussian. They could have an excellent marginal distribution but would not consistently agree across related views. A useful representation must satisfy both requirements while preserving information relevant to downstream tasks.

## What the theory does and does not establish

Read the optimality result together with its task family, probe, distributional assumptions, and constraints. A theorem about favourable feature geometry does not show that an arbitrary optimiser will discover the right visual concepts on an arbitrary dataset.

For practical evaluation, keep the encoder fixed and compare task heads under the same data split. Also inspect feature variance and covariance, and test whether changes that matter to the task remain distinguishable. These diagnostics answer different questions and should not be compressed into one “healthy representation” score.

<div class="insight-box"><strong>Key Insight:</strong> Predictive agreement couples related examples. Distribution regularisation prevents that agreement from being satisfied by an uninformative representation. The downstream evaluation must still test meaning.</div>

Compare the target-encoder approach in [I-JEPA](/blog/jepa/i-jepa/) and the contrastive training used by [VL-JEPA](/blog/jepa/vl-jepa/).
